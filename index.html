Service intervals

  • First 500 miles: change the break-in oil, then re-torque what your builder lists, plus U-bolts and lug nuts.
  • Every 3,000 miles or 6 months: oil and filter, using the oil your builder specifies. Grease the chassis, U-joints and slip yokes at the same time.
  • Every month: check oil, coolant, transmission fluid hot in Park, brake fluid, power steering fluid and tire pressure.
  • Every year: inspect belts, hoses, brake hoses, exhaust hangers and battery terminals. Check front wheel bearing play.
  • Every 2 years: coolant and brake fluid.
  • Every 30,000 miles: transmission fluid and filter, transfer case and both axle oils, front wheel bearings repacked.

General guidance for an old truck with a fresh engine. Your builder's sheet and the shop manual overrule it.

How to run this build
  1. Follow the objective card at the very top of the Build tab. It gives you one objective at a time, the steps to finish it and buttons that take you to each one. When the steps are done it moves you to the next objective on its own.
  2. Below it, This week pulls together what is in progress, what is due, shops to follow up with, parts whose return window is closing, and the next decision holding things up.
  3. Work the Build tab top to bottom. System 01 is checks only. Do those before ordering anything.
  4. Open a system and tap Guide before you talk to a shop about it. It tells you what matters, what to ask and what goes wrong.
  5. Tools I need, at the top of the Build tab, lists every tool for the jobs you marked Me. Parts on hold show a red note until you record the fact they are waiting on, and they stay off your order list until then.
  6. Every job has a How to button: difficulty level, tools, steps, what not to skip, and the best videos and write-ups I could find for your truck. If you decide to do one yourself, it flips the job from Shop to Me.
  7. Tap the square on a job to mark it in progress, tap again for done. Tap the job name to edit it.
  8. Every part is marked Required or Optional. In the catalog, filter by Required, If yours is bad, or Optional to browse the decorative parts and accessories separately. On your own list, tap the label to flip it. Totals keep required and optional money apart.
  9. On the Parts tab, switch to LMC catalog to search every catalog part that fits your truck, then tap Add to put it on your list with the part number and price filled in.
  10. Every part has a Shop online button that searches Summit, eBay, Google and RockAuto for it. The Stores view on the Parts tab lists the shops dentside owners use and what each is best for.
  11. When a part shows a Choices button, tap it to compare the options with prices and catalog pictures, then tap Choose. Your totals follow your choice, and you can change it any time. In the catalog, Add as a choice puts another option next to a part already on your list.
  12. On the Parts tab, enter the price when you find the part. Move it from Need to In cart to Purchased to Delivered to Installed. Totals update per system.
  13. Before a shop visit, open that system and tap Shop sheet. It gathers the open jobs, your truck facts and the questions to ask into one page you can share or copy.
  14. Money plan, on the Shops tab, spreads the whole build across months at the amount you can put in, and flags the big bills to save for. Log every call and visit under the shop it belongs to.
  15. Before you collect quotes, open Shops and tap Estimates. It shows the normal price range for each kind of shop work, things that commonly go wrong, and a projected total for the whole build.
  16. Find local shops, on the Shops tab, lists shops around Sierra Vista and Tucson by the kind of work they do. Each system on the Build tab has a Local shops button that jumps straight to the right ones.
  17. Add each shop you talk to on the Shops tab and record every quote, even the ones you will decline. Switch to Compare by system to see them side by side.
  18. Photograph everything before it comes apart, every casting number, and every paper quote and receipt. Photos file under the system you pick.
  19. Put permanent numbers in Truck facts on the Log tab. Put day-to-day notes and decisions in the Log itself.
  20. Save a backup from this tab after any big session. The app keeps everything on this device only.
460 quick facts
Family
Ford 385-series big block, the same family as the 429. Bore 4.36 in, stroke 3.85 in.
Firing order
1-5-4-2-6-3-7-8. The distributor turns counterclockwise.
Cylinder numbering
Passenger side is 1 to 4 front to back. Driver side is 5 to 8 front to back.
Factory fit
Ford put the 460 in 2WD trucks only from 1973 to 1979, never in a 4x4. That is why the mounts, oil pan, accessory brackets and radiator on your truck are swap parts. Many 2WD truck 460 parts, including the factory exhaust manifolds, are reported to fit the half-ton 4x4 frame.
Torque specs and clearances
Left out on purpose. They change with the fasteners and parts your builder uses. Your engine builder's sheet is the only one that counts.
